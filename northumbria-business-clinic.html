01 · Project overview

The challenge was not only creating value — it was measuring it consistently.

The project brief called for a comprehensive approach to capturing, evaluating and communicating the impact of consultancy work delivered by students for live clients through the Business Clinic programme.

Business problem

The Clinic needed a clearer, more consistent way to capture evidence after projects, understand outcomes for different stakeholders and communicate its value to a range of audiences.

Project aim

Develop a framework connecting stakeholder needs, impact evidence, longer-term follow-up, outcome evaluation and dissemination.

Project objectives

01
Audit current practice and external contextReview consultancy, higher-education engagement and knowledge-exchange approaches.
02
Understand stakeholder evidence needsConsider clients, students, academic staff, Clinic management, university leadership and the wider community.
03
Plan evidence collection and follow-upRecommend methods for capturing impact after project completion and over the longer term.
04
Evaluate and communicate impactConsider client outcomes, capability development, performance indicators and audience-appropriate dissemination.
02 · Internal audit

What was working — and what could be strengthened?

The project material highlighted an established consultancy model and positive foundations, alongside opportunities to make impact evidence more consistent and useful.

✓ Existing strengths

  • Established student-led consultancy model
  • Academic supervision supporting quality
  • Positive client feedback on project outputs
  • Student skills and employability development

↗ Areas to strengthen

  • Capture impact evidence more consistently
  • Strengthen longer-term client follow-up
  • Standardise data collection across projects
  • Build stronger evidence of business outcomes
03 · Stakeholder analysis

Different stakeholders need different evidence.

The brief explicitly considered six stakeholder groups. A useful impact framework needs to connect each group's questions to relevant evidence rather than relying on a single overall satisfaction measure.

04 · Proposed measurement logic

From project activity to evidence of impact.

The following visual summarises a practical measurement sequence aligned with the project brief. It is a presentation of the framework logic, not a claim that every measure was implemented.

STEP 01Capture a baseline

Record the client's starting point, needs and intended outcomes.

STEP 02Record delivery

Capture project outputs, feedback, engagement and agreed actions.

STEP 03Follow up

Check progress after completion and at planned longer-term intervals.

STEP 04Evaluate outcomes

Compare evidence against relevant KPIs and communicate what changed.

Potential evidence categories from the brief include business performance, turnover, capability development, client satisfaction, student confidence and employability. Specific KPI definitions and collection intervals should be confirmed against the final report.

05 · Dissemination strategy

Make the evidence useful to the audience.

The brief called for formats and channels tailored to prospective clients, students, university leadership, award applications and accreditation panels.

01Prospective clients

Short case studies, outcome summaries and client stories.

02Students

Project showcases and examples of skills developed.

03University leadership

Concise KPI dashboards and periodic impact reports.

04Award applications

Structured evidence, examples and documented outcomes.

05Accreditation panels

Consistent evidence, methodology and evaluation summaries.

06Wider community

Accessible impact highlights and partnership updates.

06 · Key takeaway

Measure consistently. Follow up meaningfully. Communicate clearly.

“The opportunity is to measure, organise and communicate the value the Business Clinic already creates more consistently.”

Central takeaway from the project material.
